# Qafila pitch narration (about 3 minutes)

Spoken by the narrator. Numbers verified on 26 Oct 2026: AfDB trade finance report (2025 edition), Afreximbank African Trade Report 2025, Chainalysis 2025 Geography of Crypto, ICC trade finance fraud estimate.

## 1. Cold open (stats)
Every year, Africa's banks turn down more than a third of the small businesses that ask them for trade finance. Only 63 percent of SME applications are approved. The gap between what importers need and what banks lend is 74 to 92 billion dollars a year. And it is not because the money is not there. In the last twelve months, Sub-Saharan Africa received more than 205 billion dollars on-chain, close to half of it in stablecoins.

## 2. The problem
So why do banks say no? Checking a ten-thousand-dollar shipment costs almost as much as checking a ten-million-dollar one. Small deals do not pay for their own due diligence. Fraud makes it worse: forged documents and the same invoice financed twice cost the industry an estimated five billion dollars a year. Many Muslim business owners also avoid interest-based credit altogether. And the last platforms that let the public co-own real assets in Africa collapsed, because nobody could verify the asset and the operator held the money.

## 3. Qafila
Qafila is a caravan on Stellar. For centuries, caravans crossing the Sahara were financed by partners who pooled money, bought goods and shared the profit of a real trade. Qafila does the same with a smart contract: it buys the goods from the supplier, owns them, and sells them to the importer at a price fixed from day one. Investors fund a specific shipment and share that fixed profit. Nothing grows with time, and late fees go to charity, never to investors.

## 4. The importer
Here is how it works for an importer in Lagos. She signs up with a passkey, no seed phrase, and uploads the pro-forma invoice, the packing list and her supplier's business licence. In under a minute, Qafila's AI extracts every key field with a quote as evidence, runs deterministic checks on codes, numbers, dates and weights, looks the supplier up in the GLEIF register, screens every party and vessel against sanctions lists, and compares prices with a reference table. A policy table decides, never the model alone, and a person signs off. Then she reads a plain-language purchase promise and signs it with her passkey. That same signature locks her security deposit: twenty percent of the cost on a first deal, falling with every on-time repayment.

## 5. The investor
For an investor, it takes thirty seconds. Scan the QR code, create a passkey wallet, tick the risk box, and fund the deal. Shares are minted one to one as compliance-locked receipts on a Stellar asset whose issuer is locked to the contract. Every investor sees the report, the live carrier events and the loss order before they confirm: the importer's deposit first, then recoveries, then investors pro rata, with Qafila's own five percent losing alongside them.

## 6. Shipment and repayment
Once the deal is funded, the contract pays the supplier's deposit and the supplier ships to Qafila's order. When the bill of lading arrives, the AI matches it against the invoice, the packing list and the carrier's loading event, an autonomous agent signs the confirmation on-chain, and the balance is paid. The importer signs the sale at the fixed price, her deposit counts as the first payment, and she repays by the due date. If she is late, a fixed fee goes to charity, and the ledger shows it. Investors claim principal plus profit as soon as the price is paid.
